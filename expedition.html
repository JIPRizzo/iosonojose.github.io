The (unfinished) why

Life is a struggle. Particularly when you’re split between places. That split has been my burden. Yet being a multiculturite is also a blessing, and perhaps my unfair advantage.

Okay, José. Whoop-t-doo. Good for you. 😮‍💨

Truth is, I don’t know.

Maybe because I can, at least technically, and I don’t take that for granted.

Maybe because this journey is a mini-version of what I’m trying to do with my life: earn a living as a storyteller while navigating the AI revolution.

Maybe because the bumpy road to fatherhood taught me just how scarce time really is.

The seed was planted around my 30th birthday. Bjarke, my Danish teacher, gave me a book documenting a Dutchman’s journey from Amsterdam to Singapore.

As the wrapping fell away, the title stopped me. Amsterdam to Singapore... on a bicycle? Wait, what? 😳

Then came the jolt of wonder. Something I hadn’t felt for the longest time.

By the time I finished it, I couldn’t stop wondering whether I could do something like that.

That was almost a decade ago. Today, I’m finally finding out.

In the months leading up to this, I’ve been collecting reasons. Reasons for leaving. But what’s the point in polishing them now? I’m more interested in noticing how the why changes as I make my way south.

Maybe, deep down, I simply want to know whether I can. Maybe this is an escape valve from my neat life in the Nordics. Maybe I won’t make it all the way. The uncertainty is certainly part of the appeal.

A moving inquiry

Europa

I’m travelling from Copenhagen to Barcelona under my own leg power. Most of my life, travel has been ticket, machine, new place. New machine this time, except I’m doing the work and moving slowly. I’m keen to experience the places between the coordinates on a map.

Strangers

I’ll converse with people I know who have made a life elsewhere. Strangers I meet on the road. Anyone willing to share their story and tell me what belonging means to them.

Broadcast

Films, field notes, field recordings and unfinished ideas will emerge while I’m moving. They’re my attempt to catch what I sense before hindsight tidies it up. The expedition is the documentary’s raw draft, shared in real time.

Where are you from?

Such a simple, frequently asked question. It used to cause me distress. Shame, even. Not because I couldn’t answer it calmly. I could. I might have looked confident, rehearsed, even funny. The calm was part of the performance.

Quick version or long? Do you mean where I was born? My country of origin? Where I consider myself from? My parents? Where I live now? Each answer is just the tip of the same massive iceberg.

What if the better question is...

Where do you belong?

Can home be multiple countries?

A place?

A language... or maybe a mixture of many?

A person?

A people?

A memory?

Something we carry and continually modify?

The question no longer unsettles me in the same way, and I’m not looking for definitive answers. I’m collecting parallels between people who left, people who stayed, the places that shaped us and the places we chose.

Simple tools. Complicated questions.
