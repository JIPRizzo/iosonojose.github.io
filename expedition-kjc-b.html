We’ve all heard it before and likely used it at some point.

where are you from?...

That question has caused me dread, distress, delight, shame, confusion.It still can.

I’ve learned to answer calmly. Over the years, I’ve experimented with all types of answers: funny ones, short ones, long ones, no answers, new answers.

I don’t take myself too seriously anymore. Contradictions and I get along much better nowadays. 😏

What has always fascinated me is how such a simple question can stir up so much.

Whether you’re a multiculturite, have parents from different backgrounds, are a third-culture kid, or simply don’t feel you belong in the place where you were born, sometimes a one- or two-word answer just feels like shortchange.

Maybe that’s a José problem. I don’t know.

The (unfinished) why

Sí, completely unnecessary. I know. Pity me. I suffer from FOMO and have convinced myself this might be a worthwhile opportunity to practise this whole filmmaker-documentarist thingy in public. Besides, I’m too young and too poor to buy a sports car to soothe my midlife crisis. Luckily, my wife Sofie and our son Valentino fully support this self-inflicted mission.

Life is a struggle. Particularly when you’re split between places. That split has been my burden. Yet being a multiculturite is also a blessing, and perhaps my unfair advantage.

Okay, José. Whoop-t-doo. Good for you. 😮‍💨

Truth is, I don’t know.

Maybe because I can, at least technically, and I don’t take that for granted.

Maybe because this journey is a mini-version of what I’m trying to do with my life: earn a living as a storyteller while navigating the AI revolution.

Maybe because the bumpy road to fatherhood taught me just how scarce time really is.

The seed was planted around my 30th birthday. Bjarke, my Danish teacher, gave me a book documenting a Dutchman’s journey from Amsterdam to Singapore.

As the wrapping fell away, the title stopped me. Amsterdam to Singapore... on a bicycle? Wait, what? 😳

Then came the jolt of wonder. Something I hadn’t felt for the longest time.

By the time I finished it, I couldn’t stop wondering whether I could do something like that.

That was almost a decade ago. Today, I’m finally finding out.

In the months leading up to this, I’ve been collecting reasons. Reasons for leaving. But what’s the point in polishing them now? I’m more interested in noticing how the why changes as I make my way south.

Maybe, deep down, I simply want to know whether I can. Maybe this is an escape valve from my neat life in the Nordics. Maybe I won’t make it all the way. The uncertainty is certainly part of the appeal.

A moving inquiry

Europa

I’m travelling from Copenhagen to Barcelona under my own leg power. Most of my life, travel has been ticket, machine, new place. New machine this time, except I’m doing the work and moving slowly. The direction is relatively clear. It’s the journey that’s the destination.

Strangers

I’ll converse with people I know who have made a life elsewhere. Strangers I meet on the road. Anyone, really, willing to share their story... and listen to me babble? 😅

Broadcast

Films, field notes and recordings will emerge while I’m moving. They’re my attempt to catch what I sense before hindsight tidies it up. This is an experiment in building in public. It’s my first time “bikepacking.” So I’m all in.
